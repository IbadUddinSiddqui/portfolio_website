# Film Plotting and Darkroom Processing

## Overview

Film plotting and darkroom processing are photolithographic techniques used to transfer PCB pattern designs onto copper-clad boards. This process creates the etch-resistant pattern that defines copper traces, pads, and other features on the PCB. This laboratory session covers generating photo-tools from digital designs, applying photoresist, UV exposure, and pattern development.

## Objective

To generate film positives from PCB layout files (Gerber data), prepare copper-clad boards with liquid or dry-film photoresist, perform UV exposure to transfer patterns, develop the exposed photoresist to reveal the circuit pattern, and inspect developed boards for pattern quality.

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

- **Photoresist**: A light-sensitive polymer that changes solubility when exposed to UV light. Positive photoresist becomes more soluble when exposed; negative photoresist becomes less soluble.
- **Photo-tool**: A transparent film with the circuit pattern printed in opaque ink. The dark areas block UV light during exposure.
- **UV Exposure**: The process of transferring the photo-tool pattern to the photoresist-coated board using ultraviolet light. Exposure time depends on UV intensity and photoresist sensitivity.
- **Development**: The chemical process that removes either exposed (positive resist) or unexposed (negative resist) photoresist areas, revealing the copper beneath.
- **Resolution**: The minimum feature size reliably reproducible. Contact printing (film in contact with board) achieves 0.1mm feature resolution.

## Project / Task

Generate film positives for the LED driver PCB design, coat a copper-clad board with photoresist, expose using the film positive, develop the pattern, and inspect resolution quality.

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Generate Film Positives

1. Open Gerber files in a CAM viewer
2. Set up film output:
   - Select the bottom copper layer
   - Set mirror (if required for bottom-side exposure)
   - Print resolution: 2400 DPI minimum
3. Print to transparency film:
   - Load film with emulsion side determined by printer
   - Print a test pattern first to verify density
   - Print the final film positive
4. Inspect film:
   - Verify optical density (opaque areas)
   - Check for pinholes or scratches
   - Measure registration marks

### Step 2: Clean Copper-Clad Board

1. Cut copper-clad board to size (50mm × 40mm)
2. Clean with scouring pad and detergent
3. Rinse with water
4. Degrease with acetone or isopropyl alcohol
5. Handle edges only — avoid touching the copper surface

### Step 3: Apply Photoresist

1. For liquid photoresist:
   - Spray or dip-coat the clean board
   - Apply even coating
   - Dry in dark cabinet at 70°C for 15 minutes
2. For dry-film photoresist:
   - Cut film slightly larger than board
   - Remove protective liner
   - Laminator at 110°C
   - Apply with roller to remove air bubbles

### Step 4: UV Exposure

1. Place the film positive on the photoresist-coated board:
   - Emulsion-to-emulsion contact for sharpest resolution
   - Align registration marks
2. Place in UV exposure unit:
   - Glass plate on top for pressure
   - Set exposure timer
   - Typical exposure: 60-120 seconds at 10 mW/cm²
3. Expose the board to UV light
4. Remove and prepare for development

### Step 5: Develop the Pattern

1. Prepare developer solution:
   - For positive resist: Sodium hydroxide (1-2%) or commercial developer
   - For negative resist: Specific solvent-based developer
2. Immerse exposed board in developer:
   - Temperature: 25-30°C
   - Development time: 30-60 seconds
   - Gentle agitation
3. Rinse in water immediately
4. Inspect the developed pattern:
   - Clear traces should be visible (positive resist)
   - No residual photoresist in copper areas
   - Clean, well-defined edges

### Step 6: Inspection and Touch-Up

1. Inspect under magnification:
   - Check for complete development
   - Look for pinholes in the photoresist
   - Verify trace widths match design
2. Touch up defects:
   - Use fine brush and photoresist for missing areas
   - Use fine scalpel for excess photoresist
3. Post-bake at 120°C for 15 minutes to harden photoresist

## What Was Done

Generated film positives for the LED driver PCB at 2400 DPI resolution with proper optical density. Cleaned and photoresist-coated a copper-clad board, exposed using the film positive in a UV exposure unit for 90 seconds, and developed the pattern to reveal clean, well-defined traces with 0.3mm minimum feature resolution. The developed board was inspected and shows no pinholes or defects in the circuit pattern.
