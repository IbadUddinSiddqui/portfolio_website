# Desoldering and Rework Techniques

## Overview

Desoldering is the process of removing solder from a joint to release and replace components. It is an essential skill for PCB repair, rework, and component recycling. This laboratory session covers both through-hole and surface-mount desoldering techniques using various tools including solder wick, desoldering pumps, and hot air rework stations.

## Objective

To master desoldering techniques using solder wick and desoldering pump, remove through-hole components without damaging PCB pads, remove surface-mount components using hot air, clean and prepare PCB pads for new component installation, and perform complete component replacement (rework) with quality inspection.

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

Desoldering requires controlled heating and removal of molten solder. Key concepts include:

- **Solder Wick (Braid)**: Braided copper wire that uses capillary action to absorb molten solder. The wick must be fresh (not oxidized) and used with flux.
- **Desoldering Pump (Solder Sucker)**: A vacuum tool that mechanically removes molten solder. Requires one-handed operation while heating the joint with the iron.
- **Hot Air Rework**: Uses controlled hot air to simultaneously melt all connections on an SMD component, allowing removal without mechanical stress.
- **Pad Damage Prevention**: Excessive heat or mechanical force can lift PCB pads. Maximum soldering iron tip temperature for rework: 370°C. Maximum heating time: 5 seconds per attempt with 10-second cooldown.

## Project / Task

Remove three through-hole resistors and one DIP-8 IC socket from a practice PCB using desoldering techniques, clean the pads, and install replacement components. Inspect for pad damage and verify all new solder joints meet IPC standards.

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Setup for Desoldering

1. Clean the soldering iron tip and set temperature to 350°C
2. Prepare desoldering pump: Check plunger spring and clean nozzle
3. Cut a 50mm length of solder wick for each joint
4. Apply flux to the solder wick if not pre-fluxed
5. Position the PCB securely in a holder

### Step 2: Through-Hole Desoldering with Solder Pump

1. Heat the solder joint with the iron for 2-3 seconds
2. Position the desoldering pump nozzle over the molten solder
3. Activate the pump to remove solder
4. Repeat if necessary — most joints require 2-3 passes
5. After removing bulk solder, use solder wick for cleanup

### Step 3: Through-Hole Desoldering with Solder Wick

1. Place solder wick over the joint to be desoldered
2. Press the iron tip onto the wick, sandwiching it against the joint
3. Wait 2-3 seconds for the wick to heat and absorb the solder
4. Lift the iron and wick simultaneously
5. Cut off the used portion of wick
6. Repeat with fresh wick if solder remains

### Step 4: Remove Through-Hole Component

1. After desoldering all pins, verify no solder remains
2. Gently pull the component from the top of the PCB
3. If the component does not release, re-heat and wick the stubborn joints
4. Never force a component — this will lift pads

### Step 5: Hot Air SMD Removal

1. Select the appropriate hot air nozzle for the component
2. Set hot air temperature to 350°C and airflow to medium
3. Apply flux around the component leads
4. Heat the component evenly with circular motion for 10-20 seconds
5. When solder melts, gently lift the component with tweezers
6. Remove component and turn off hot air

### Step 6: Pad Cleaning and Preparation

1. Clean remaining solder from pads using solder wick and flux
2. Inspect pads under magnification for:
   - Lifted or damaged pads
   - Burnt or delaminated board material
   - Remaining solder or debris
3. Clean the area with isopropyl alcohol and a brush
4. Apply fresh flux to pads before installing new component

### Step 7: Install Replacement Component

1. Insert the new component into the cleaned holes/pads
2. Solder using the five-step method from Lab 08
3. Trim excess leads for through-hole components
4. Clean flux residue

## What Was Done

Successfully removed three through-hole resistors and one DIP-8 IC socket from a practice PCB using a combination of desoldering pump and solder wick techniques. No pads were damaged during the process. The pads were cleaned and prepared, and replacement components were installed with proper solder joints meeting IPC-A-610 acceptance criteria. All rework was completed within 20 minutes.
