# Diode Limiters (Clipper Circuits)

## Overview

Diode limiter circuits, also known as clippers, are used to remove or clip portions of an input signal above or below a specified voltage level. They are essential for waveform shaping, signal conditioning, and overvoltage protection. This laboratory session covers both series and shunt diode limiter configurations.

## Objective

To practice the implementation and testing of diode limiter (clipper) circuits in series and shunt configurations.

## Domain

Waveform Shaping — Signal Conditioning and Protection Circuits.

## CLO and Taxonomy Level

- **CLO-3**: Implement and analyze diode limiter circuits for waveform shaping.
- **Taxonomy Level**: P3 — Supervised Practice (Practical application of limiter circuits under guidance).

## Components and Equipment

- **Semiconductors**: 1N4007 diodes (2)
- **Passive Components**: 1kΩ resistor, 10kΩ resistor
- **Equipment**: Function generator, DC power supply, Oscilloscope, DMM

## Required Skills

- Diode forward/reverse bias behavior
- Understanding of signal waveforms

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

- **Series Limiter**: The diode is in series with the load. The diode conducts only when the input voltage exceeds the diode's forward voltage (≈0.7V for silicon), clipping the negative portion of the input signal.
- **Shunt Limiter**: The diode is connected in parallel (shunt) with the load, with a bias voltage. When the input exceeds the bias voltage + 0.7V, the diode conducts and clips the signal.
- **Biased Limiter**: By adding a DC bias voltage, the clipping level can be adjusted to any desired voltage.
- **Applications**: Overvoltage protection, amplitude limiting, waveform shaping, noise removal.

## Circuit Configuration

Two configurations are tested:
- **Series Positive Clipper**: Diode in series with signal path, clips negative half-cycles
- **Shunt Positive Clipper**: Diode in shunt with load, clips above bias voltage

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Series Limiter

1. Connect the diode in series between the function generator and load resistor
2. Set the function generator to output a 5Vpp sine wave at 1kHz
3. Observe the output across the load using the oscilloscope
4. Note which portion of the waveform is clipped

### Step 2: Shunt Limiter

1. Connect the diode in parallel with the load resistor
2. Add a DC bias voltage source in series with the diode
3. Observe the clipping action above the bias voltage + 0.7V
4. Vary the bias voltage and observe changes in clipping level

### Step 3: Symmetrical Clipping

1. Use two back-to-back diodes (Zener or regular diodes) to clip both positive and negative peaks
2. Observe the symmetrical waveform limiting

## What Was Done

Implemented series and shunt diode limiter circuits to clip specific parts of an input signal waveform. Tested with sine wave input at various bias voltages and observed the clipping action.

## What Was Learnt

- Limiters are effective for protecting circuits from over-voltage or for shaping waveforms
- Series limiters remove entire half-cycles; shunt limiters clip above adjustable thresholds
- Biased limiters allow precise control of the clipping voltage
- Back-to-back Zener diodes provide symmetrical bipolar clipping

## Engineering Applications

- Overvoltage protection in sensitive electronic circuits
- Amplitude limiting in communication receivers
- Signal conditioning for data acquisition systems
- Audio distortion and effect circuits
- Square wave generation from sine waves
