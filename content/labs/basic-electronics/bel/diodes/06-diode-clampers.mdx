# Diode Clampers (DC Level Shifters)

## Overview

Diode clamper circuits shift the entire AC waveform to a different DC level without changing its shape or amplitude. Unlike limiters (clippers) that remove portions of the waveform, clampers simply add or subtract a DC offset. This laboratory session covers both positive and negative clamper configurations.

## Objective

To practice diode clamper circuits for DC level shifting of AC signals.

## Domain

Waveform Shaping — Signal Conditioning and DC Restoration.

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

- **Positive Clamper**: Shifts the entire waveform so that the negative peak is clamped to 0V (or a reference voltage), making the entire waveform positive.
- **Negative Clamper**: Shifts the entire waveform so that the positive peak is clamped to 0V, making the entire waveform negative.
- **Biased Clamper**: Uses an additional DC voltage source to clamp the signal at a specific DC level other than 0V.
- **Time Constant**: The RC time constant (τ = RC) must be much larger than the signal period (T) for proper clamping operation. A large time constant ensures the capacitor holds its charge between cycles.
- **Clamper vs. Clipper**: Clampers shift the entire waveform; clippers remove portions of the waveform.

## Circuit Configuration

The basic clamper consists of:
- Capacitor in series with the signal path
- Diode and resistor connected from the signal path to ground/reference
- Optional DC bias for level adjustment

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Positive Clamper

1. Connect the capacitor in series with the signal input
2. Connect the diode cathode to the capacitor output and anode to ground
3. Connect the resistor from the capacitor output to ground
4. Apply a 5Vpp, 1kHz sine wave input
5. Observe the output — the negative peaks should be at approximately 0V

### Step 2: Negative Clamper

1. Reverse the diode direction (anode to signal path, cathode to ground)
2. Observe the output — the positive peaks should be at approximately 0V

### Step 3: Biased Clamper

1. Add a DC bias voltage in series with the diode
2. Observe the clamping level shift according to the bias voltage

## What Was Learnt

- Clampers shift the entire waveform to a different DC level without altering the shape

- Biased clampers allow precise control of the DC reference level
