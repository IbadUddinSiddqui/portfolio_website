# Bridge Rectifier and Capacitive Filtering

## Overview

The bridge rectifier is the most widely used rectifier configuration, providing full-wave rectification without requiring a center-tapped transformer. Adding a capacitor filter dramatically improves the output quality by reducing ripple voltage. This laboratory session covers bridge rectifier operation both with and without capacitive filtering.

## Objective

To practice bridge rectifier circuits both with and without capacitive filtering.

## Domain

Circuit Analysis — Full-Wave Rectification and Filtering.

## CLO and Taxonomy Level

- **CLO-3**: Analyze and construct bridge rectifier circuits with capacitive filtering.
- **Taxonomy Level**: P3 — Supervised Practice (Practical application of bridge rectifier under guidance).

## Components and Equipment

- **Semiconductors**: 1N4007 diodes (4)
- **Passive Components**: 1kΩ load resistor, 100µF electrolytic capacitor, 470µF electrolytic capacitor
- **Equipment**: Step-down transformer (12V), Oscilloscope, DMM

## Required Skills

- Diode operation and orientation identification
- Full-wave rectification concepts

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

- **Bridge Rectifier**: Four diodes arranged in a bridge configuration. During the positive half-cycle, diodes D1 and D3 conduct; during the negative half-cycle, diodes D2 and D4 conduct. Current through the load flows in the same direction during both cycles.
- **Capacitive Filtering**: A capacitor placed across the load charges to the peak voltage during the conduction period and discharges through the load during the non-conduction period, reducing the ripple.
- **Ripple Voltage Formula**: Vr = Iload / (f × C), where f is the ripple frequency (2 × input frequency for full-wave).
- **Advantages over Center-Tapped**: No center-tapped transformer needed, lower PIV requirement per diode.

## Circuit Configuration

The bridge rectifier consists of:
- Four diodes arranged in a bridge (D1, D2, D3, D4)
- AC input across the bridge AC terminals
- Load connected across the bridge DC output terminals
- Optional filter capacitor in parallel with the load

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Bridge Rectifier Without Filter

1. Construct the bridge configuration on the breadboard
2. Connect AC input to the bridge AC terminals
3. Connect the load resistor across the DC output terminals
4. Observe and sketch the output waveform

### Step 2: Bridge Rectifier With Capacitive Filter

1. Add a 100µF electrolytic capacitor across the load (observe polarity)
2. Observe the output waveform — note the reduced ripple
3. Replace with 470µF capacitor and observe the further ripple reduction
4. Measure DC output voltage for both capacitor values

### Step 3: Measurements

1. Measure the ripple voltage (peak-to-peak) for each capacitor value
2. Measure the DC output voltage for each configuration
3. Calculate the ripple factor: RF = Vr(pp) / Vdc

## What Was Done

Assembled a bridge rectifier circuit using four 1N4007 diodes and tested output waveforms both with and without a shunt capacitor filter. Observed the effect of different capacitor values (100µF and 470µF) on ripple reduction.

## Results and Observations

| Configuration | DC Output (V) | Ripple Voltage (Vpp) | Ripple Factor |
|---------------|---------------|---------------------|---------------|
| No Filter | ≈5.1 V | High | ≈1.21 |
| 100µF Filter | ≈6.2 V | ≈0.8 V | ≈0.13 |
| 470µF Filter | ≈6.5 V | ≈0.2 V | ≈0.03 |

## What Was Learnt

- The capacitor filter acts as a reservoir, significantly reducing the ripple voltage
- Larger capacitor values provide better filtering (lower ripple) but increase cost and size
- The bridge rectifier provides full-wave rectification without a center-tapped transformer
- The no-filter output has high ripple; adding a filter produces a smoother DC output
