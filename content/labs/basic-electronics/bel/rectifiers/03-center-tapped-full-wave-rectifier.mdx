# Center-Tapped Full-Wave Rectifier

## Overview

The center-tapped full-wave rectifier improves upon the half-wave design by utilizing both halves of the AC cycle. Using two diodes and a center-tapped transformer, this configuration provides a higher average DC output voltage and lower ripple frequency compared to the half-wave rectifier.

## Objective

To practice the center-tapped full-wave rectifier configuration using a center-tapped transformer and two diodes.

## Domain

Circuit Analysis — Full-Wave Rectification.

## Components and Equipment

- **Semiconductors**: 1N4007 diodes (2)
- **Passive Components**: 1kΩ load resistor
- **Equipment**: Center-tapped transformer (12-0-12V), Oscilloscope, DMM
- **Tools**: Breadboard, Jumper wires, Wire strippers

## Required Skills

- Half-wave rectifier operation (Lab 02)
- Understanding of transformer center-tap configuration

## Theory and Engineering Concepts

- **Full-Wave Operation**: Two diodes conduct on alternate half-cycles, allowing current through the load during both positive and negative input cycles.
- **Center-Tapped Transformer**: Provides two equal voltage signals 180° out of phase relative to the center tap (ground reference).
- **Average DC Output**: Vdc = 2Vm/π ≈ 0.636 × Vm (double that of half-wave rectifier).
- **Ripple Frequency**: 2 × input frequency (e.g., 100Hz for 50Hz input).
- **Peak Inverse Voltage**: Each diode must withstand PIV = 2Vm.

## Circuit Configuration

The center-tapped full-wave rectifier consists of:
- Center-tapped transformer with secondary windings
- Diode D1 connected from top secondary to load
- Diode D2 connected from bottom secondary to load
- Load resistor connected between diodes' cathodes and ground (center tap)

Signal flow: Positive half-cycle → D1 conducts; Negative half-cycle → D2 conducts

## Procedure / Implementation

### Step 1: Circuit Construction

1. Identify the center-tapped transformer terminals (top winding, center tap, bottom winding)
2. Mount both diodes on the breadboard with correct orientation
3. Connect:
   - Diode D1 anode to the top transformer secondary
   - Diode D2 anode to the bottom transformer secondary
   - Both diode cathodes connected together to one end of the load resistor
   - Load resistor's other end to the transformer center tap (ground)
4. Verify all connections before applying power

### Step 2: Testing and Measurement

1. Connect the oscilloscope Channel 1 to the load output
2. Set the oscilloscope to DC coupling for DC level observation
3. Apply AC power to the transformer primary
4. Observe the output waveform — should show both positive half-cycles
5. Measure the DC output voltage using the DMM

## What Was Done

Built a full-wave rectifier using a center-tapped transformer and two 1N4007 diodes. The output waveform showed both positive half-cycles of the AC input, producing a pulsating DC output with double the ripple frequency of the half-wave rectifier.

## What Was Learnt

- By utilizing both halves of the AC cycle, the average DC output voltage is higher compared to a half-wave rectifier
- The center-tapped full-wave rectifier requires a center-tapped transformer, adding cost and size
- The ripple frequency doubles to 100Hz, making filtering easier
- Each diode experiences a PIV of 2Vm
